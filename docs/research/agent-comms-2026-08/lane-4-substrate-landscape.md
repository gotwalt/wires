# Lane 4 — Technical Substrate Landscape: Encrypted Group Messaging & Decentralized Comms

*Research agent output, verbatim. Current to 2026-08-15. Part of the [agent-comms market research](./synthesis.md).*

## 1. MLS (RFC 9420)

**Adoption — 2025-26 was the inflection, but every win is centralized:**
- GSMA adopted MLS as the E2EE layer of RCS Universal Profile 3.0 (March 2025), with Google and Apple both committed (https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/article/rcs-encryption-a-leap-towards-secure-and-interoperable-messaging/). Cross-platform iPhone↔Android E2EE RCS began rolling out **May 11, 2026** (iOS 26.5+, on-by-default where supported) (https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/android-ios-end-to-end-encrypted-rcs-messaging/). GSMA calls RCS "the first large-scale messaging service with interoperable E2EE across providers."
- **Wire**: first to make MLS its *default* — GA April 2025, Proteus migration essentially complete mid-2025 (https://support.wire.com/hc/en-us/articles/18666343787293-Migration-from-Proteus-to-MLS-protocol); crypto is Rust `wireapp/core-crypto`.
- **Discord DAVE**: MLS for group key agreement on voice/video; E2EE-by-default migration for eligible calls completed ~March 2026, including bots as E2EE session members (https://daveprotocol.com/, https://www.cyberkendra.com/2026/05/discord-calls-are-now-end-to-end.html). Text chat still not E2EE.
- **Cisco Webex**: longest-running production MLS (zero-trust meetings, MLS + SFrame) (https://www.webex.com/content/dam/wbx/us/documents/pdf/Zero_Trust_Security_White_Paper.pdf). **AWS** maintains `mls-rs` (RFC-conformant, FIPS-capable, **not third-party audited**) (https://github.com/awslabs/mls-rs).
- iMessage stays on Apple's own PQ3, not MLS. Signal and WhatsApp are conspicuously absent from MLS.
- Germany: "BSI TR-03352" could **not be verified** (not in BSI's TR index); what's real is BSI's messenger reports pointing to MLS for interoperable E2EE (https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/BSI/Publikationen/DVS-Berichte/messenger.pdf) and Germany's Sovereign Tech Agency funding the OpenMLS audit.

**Tooling:** OpenMLS is the de-facto reference (v0.8.1 Feb 2025; independent SRLabs audit — 8 findings, 1 High, fixed; https://blog.phnx.im/openmls-independent-security-audit/). It ships **fork-resolution helpers** because state forks are common enough in production to need them (https://cryspen.com/post/mls-fork-resolution/). MIMI (IETF) is effectively standardizing the delivery service as a per-room "hub provider" that sequences MLS handshake traffic — drafts active but slipped, nothing at IESG (https://datatracker.ietf.org/group/mimi/about/).

**Operational pain points (the load-bearing facts):**
- MLS **requires a central sequencer**: exactly one commit per epoch; servers reject losing commits and force client retries (RFC 9750, https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9750.html; https://blog.phnx.im/making-mls-more-decentralized/). MSC4244 notes MLS "is optimized for cases where the server guarantees messages are delivered in-order… without loss" — a direct barrier to gossip/eventually-consistent transports (https://github.com/matrix-org/matrix-spec-proposals/blob/travis/msc/mls/00-core/proposals/4244-rfc9420-mls-for-matrix.md).
- Decentralized MLS is still research-grade: Phoenix R&D's DMLS keeps a DAG of epochs + puncturable PRFs, trading storage/FS complexity for ordering freedom (https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-kohbrok-mls-dmls-01.html); Germ restricts to pairwise groups to dodge ordering entirely (https://www.germnetwork.com/blog/decentralized-pairwise-mls). Matrix has been "investigating" MLS since 2023 with no shipped client (http://arewemlsyet.com/).
- The ecosystem gives you "only client-side MLS logic" — DS, key-package directory, state persistence, reorder buffers, fork recovery are all DIY, "the 20% of surface area that takes 80% of the effort" (Nov 2025 engineering investigation: https://dr-knz.net/mls-investigation.html).
- Multi-device: each device is a separate leaf; no first-class user concept (RFC 9750). The DS necessarily sees membership graph, epoch cadence, sender activity.
- MLS + agents: **XMTP is the shipping example of non-human MLS leaf nodes** (agent SDK, agents in E2EE group chats: https://docs.xmtp.org/agents/get-started/intro); no IETF work on agent identity/credentials in MLS leaves.

## 2. Matrix / Element

- **Funding is existential and chronic**: Feb 2025 "crossroads" post — 2024 revenue $561K vs $1.2M expenses (https://matrix.org/blog/2025/02/crossroads/); paid premium matrix.org accounts introduced June 2025 (the flagship homeserver alone was ~50% of Foundation spend); Slack bridge retired Nov 2025; Dec 2025: membership doubled to ~20 orgs but **still not self-sustaining** (https://matrix.org/blog/2025/12/24/matrix-holiday-special/).
- **Element pivoted to governments**: AGPL relicense to force integrators to pay (https://element.io/blog/element-to-adopt-agplv3/); business is now Tchap (300K+ French civil servants), Bundeswehr, NATO's NI2CE, 25+ countries claimed (https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2026/02/09/matrix-messaging-gaining-ground-in-government-it/). This *proves paid demand for sovereign E2EE group comms*.
- **Protocol complexity is a decade-long bug farm**: state resolution "state resets" forced a coordinated July 2025 security fix across all server implementations and the multi-phase Project Hydra rewrite (https://matrix.org/blog/2025/08/project-hydra-improving-state-res/). Metadata (who/when/what-room) is plaintext to every participating homeserver. Sliding-sync performance regressed from early demos per the Foundation's own Holiday Special.
- **Bots/bridges**: appservice API = privileged homeserver-registered services that can mass-puppet users (https://spec.matrix.org/latest/application-service-api/) — the best existing pattern for non-human participants, but it sits **outside the E2EE trust boundary** (homeserver-granted power). Beeper relaunched July 2025 with on-device bridges ($9.99-49.99 tiers) (https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/16/beepers-all-in-one-messaging-app-relaunches-with-an-on-device-model-and-premium-upgrades/).
- **AI-agent story: essentially none.** The Foundation's 2025 review mentions LLMs only as a T&S abuse risk; no notable MSCs; FOSDEM 2025/2026 had no Matrix-agents talk. Grassroots only: baibot, community Matrix-MCP servers (https://github.com/mjknowles/matrix-mcp-server), MindRoom (https://docs.mindroom.chat/). The agent lane on Matrix is empty despite MCP-era demand.

## 3. Web3-adjacent messaging

**XMTP** — closest on paper to "E2EE groups for humans+agents":
- v3 is MLS-based (libxmtp, OpenMLS fork, NCC-assessed Dec 2024) over a purpose-built node network (https://xmtp.org/decentralization). Decentralization mostly aspirational: Dec 2025 testnet relaunch had **3 nodes**, target **7 permissioned nodes at mainnet** (~March 2026) (https://blog.xmtp.org/xmtps-decentralization-update-jan-2026/). No token.
- Real numbers are small: ~228M total network messages in ~7 months, only ~103M chat; World App's World Chat added 23.4M in a month (https://blog.xmtp.org/xmtp-network-metrics-jan-6th-2026/). Agents are the explicit positioning — Base App treats agents as "first-class chat participants that hold and move funds" (https://docs.base.org/base-app/guides/chat-agents); flagship agent Bankr: 20k+ DMs/groups in beta (https://bankr.bot/agents). Real, but crypto-trading concierge scale.

**Farcaster** — collapsed: ~40-60k DAU vs 100k peak; protocol revenue Q4 2025 down 85% YoY; **Neynar acquired Farcaster Jan 2026** (https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2025/10/28/farcaster-in-2025-the-protocol-paradox/). DMs are off-protocol and centralized (https://github.com/farcasterxyz/protocol/discussions/99). Its "agent wave" (Clanker, aethernet) was memecoin-deployment bots on public feeds, not comms.

**Nostr** — tiny usage (~thousands of DAU posting, zaps down ~20% YoY: https://www.glukhov.org/post/2025/10/nostr-overview-and-statistics/); NIP-90 Data Vending Machines stalled (DVMDash historical data ends Feb 2025: https://github.com/dtdannen/dvmdash). But two things matter:
- **Marmot protocol / NIP-EE**: MLS group messaging over Nostr identity+relays (https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot); reference stack MDK (Rust, **shipping agent-integration features**) + whitenoise-rs; White Noise app v0.3.0 Feb 2026; Amethyst PRs landed April 2026. Pre-traction, but the most serious open "MLS groups on sovereign identity" effort.
- **Block's Buzz** (see §7) is Nostr-based.

**Others**: SimpleX is the honest-metrics standout (430k+ MAU, ~20M msgs/day, Dorsey-funded, $45M valuation crowd round: https://wefunder.com/simplexchat) — no agent story. Session nearly died April 2026 (https://cyberinsider.com/session-avoids-shutdown-as-community-donations-save-the-project/). Bluesky DMs are centralized/off-protocol; E2EE deferred, team eyeing MLS (https://docs.bsky.app/blog/protocol-checkin-fall-2025). Towns shipped a token, not usage.

## 4. iroh ecosystem

- **iroh 1.0 shipped June 15, 2026** — wire-protocol stability across v1.x, restored Python/Node/Swift/Kotlin bindings, custom transports (BLE/LoRa/Tor) (https://www.iroh.computer/blog/v1). n0 raised ~$4.3M Oct 2025 (~$8.3M total); business model is authenticated relays-as-a-service (Free/Pro/Enterprise: https://www.iroh.computer/pricing). Claim: 200M+ endpoints created on public relays in 30 days. **Deadline: free public-relay support for v0.35.x ends Dec 31, 2026; earlier versions Sept 30, 2026** — the wires 1.0.3 pin is on the right side of this, but plan for the paid/self-hosted relay era.
- Production users: Delta Chat (WebXDC realtime), Fedimint 0.7 federation transport, Weird, sendme/dumbpipe; **the biggest are AI infra** — Nous Research's Psyche distributed-training network (5 self-run relays, claimed largest distributed pre-training run: https://www.iroh.computer/solutions/nous), Prime Intellect. Fuchsia rumor: unconfirmed.
- **iroh-gossip is NOT 1.0** (0.101.0, versioned in lockstep but explicitly unstabilized): HyParView+PlumTree, ~few-thousand peers per topic, **no delivery guarantees, no ordering, and no swarm auth — anyone with the 32-byte TopicId can join**; n0 explicitly punts authorization to the app layer (https://docs.iroh.computer/connecting/gossip, https://github.com/n0-computer/iroh/discussions/3168). iroh-willow is stalled at 0.0.1; iroh-docs is not a 1.0-class citizen.
- vs libp2p: consistent 2025-26 sentiment favors iroh for NAT-traversal success and simplicity (https://www.ark-builders.dev/blog/p2p-networking-webrtc-libp2p-iroh/); libp2p keeps blockchains.
- Agents on iroh: n0's 1.0 post name-checks "talk to agents"; third parties write "MCP needs a transport layer" pieces almost verbatim to the Wires pitch (https://dev.to/artem_a/ai-agent-networking-in-2026-nat-traversal-encrypted-tunnels-and-why-mcp-needs-a-transport-layer-5hbm); concrete projects (Codux, Kith with MCP integration) are small. **The authenticated-group-membership-over-iroh slot is unclaimed, and nothing on n0's roadmap sherlocks it.**
- n0 published FROST threshold signatures for iroh keys (https://www.iroh.computer/blog/frost-threshold-signatures) — relevant for roster identities that survive device loss.

## 5. Local-first

- Automerge 3.0 (July 2025) made CRDT docs production-grade on memory/size (https://automerge.org/blog/automerge-3/). Electric SQL was **acquired into Databricks/Neon's "Lakebase for apps and agents"** (Aug 2026, https://electric.ax/blog); PowerSync stayed independent and is harvesting refugees; both camps re-marketed around agents. DXOS made an explicit agentic turn ("Agents are DXOS instances running without an attached UI" + managed agent hosting: https://blog.dxos.org/announcing-the-managed-agent-service/). Jazz's founder talk: "How Local-First is accidentally perfect for the AI age."
- **Keyhive/BeeKEM (Ink & Switch) is the closest prior art to the wires design**: capability-granted membership + a CGKA (TreeKEM-inspired) redesigned for **no central sequencer** — causal partial order instead of MLS's serialized epochs, κ retained-secrets parameter trading forward secrecy against partition tolerance, "cross-fork security" (https://www.inkandswitch.com/keyhive/notebook/06/). Beelay syncs E2EE payloads only with Keyhive-granted replicas (https://github.com/automerge/beelay/blob/main/docs/protocol.md). **Status: pre-alpha, unaudited, still design-in-progress at FOSDEM 2026.** Read it in full — it validates the roster+epoch architecture while showing the cost of full concurrency support (the wires serialized membership log is simpler and closer to shippable).
- CRDT chat exists: Roomy (muni-town) = Discord-style chat on ATProto identity + Automerge, planning to adopt Keyhive for E2EE groups (https://lobste.rs/s/oqfmhj/). Ink & Switch's "Malleable Software" essay (June 2025) argues AI tools gain collaboration "for free" inside local-first substrates (https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/malleable-software/). **Nobody in local-first has shipped agents as authenticated members of an E2EE group.**

## 6. Signal

- **No bot API, by doctrine not omission**: every real Signal bot runs the full ratchet itself via unofficial clients (signal-cli et al., https://github.com/exquo/signal-soft); Moxie's 2016 "use our source, not our servers" posture still holds.
- **Whittaker's talks (strategically important)**: SXSW March 2025 — agentic AI is like "putting your brain in a jar"; integrating agents into a messenger "would undermine the privacy of your messages" (https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/07/signal-president-meredith-whittaker-calls-out-agentic-ai-as-having-profound-security-and-privacy-issues). Bloomberg Weekend interview June 9, 2026 — agents' defining features are maximal data access + independent action; **prompt injection named as the likeliest first exploit path against encrypted messengers** (https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-meredith-whittaker-weekend-interview/). Copilot-style agents reading chats are "a kind of a backdoor" to E2EE (https://thenextweb.com/news/signal-whittaker-ai-chatbots-not-friends-copilot-backdoor-privacy). Also: "Signal president warns AI agents are making encryption irrelevant" (https://cyberinsider.com/signal-president-warns-ai-agents-are-making-encryption-irrelevant/).
- Crypto evolution: Oct 2025 **SPQR "Triple Ratchet"** — ML-KEM post-quantum ratcheting composed with Double Ratchet (https://signal.org/blog/spqr/).
- Structural point: in honest E2EE there is no server-side place to "add a bot" — an agent must be a full cryptographic peer holding keys, running the ratchet, visible in the roster. Signal will not build this; Whittaker's line makes agent-access-to-plaintext the threat model itself.

## 7. The honest question: does p2p + E2EE matter when members include cloud-hosted agents?

**The competition that actually shipped (no E2EE):**
- **Block's Buzz** (July 21, 2026): Dorsey's "Slack+GitHub for teams and their AI agents," Nostr-based, Apache-2.0, **27.6k GitHub stars within a month** (https://github.com/block/buzz; https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/jack-dorsey-is-taking-on-slack-with-buzz-a-group-chat-platform-for-teams-and-their-ai-agents/). Every human and agent holds its own Schnorr keypair; hash-chain audit trail of signed events; agents take CLI actions, create channels, update their own memories. **But: one authoritative relay per workspace ("single source of truth"), and no E2EE anywhere** — NIPs used are 01/42/34/98, no NIP-44/Marmot, no encryption roadmap items (https://decrypt.co/374026/jack-dorseys-block-launches-buzz-a-nostr-based-slack-and-github-rival-for-ai-agents). Buzz chose signed-plaintext-on-a-sovereign-relay: identity and auditability, not confidentiality.
- **Paradigm's Centaur** (open-sourced May 21, 2026, 2.0 rewritten in Rust): "multiplayer, self-hosted, secure agents" living in Slack, isolated K8s sandboxes, credentials without seeing raw secrets (https://www.paradigm.xyz/2026/05/open-sourcing-centaur-multiplayer-self-hosted-secure-agents). Thesis: enterprises won't leave Slack; self-hosting = the security story.
- **Google A2A / Linux Foundation** (v1.0 in 2026, 100+ companies): the mainstream agent-interop answer is HTTP/JSON-RPC + OAuth/JWT/mTLS — **transport security only, no application-layer E2EE, no group semantics** (https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-launches-the-agent2agent-protocol-project-to-enable-secure-intelligent-communication-between-ai-agents).
- OpenAI shipped group chats in ChatGPT (humans + the model in one thread, https://openai.com/index/group-chats-in-chatgpt/) and Frontier for enterprise agents — cleartext-to-provider by construction.

**Arguments E2EE-for-agents is real:**
- The Knodel/Fábrega/Scheffler et al. paper "How To Think About E2EE and AI" (Dec 2024, rev. Mar 2025) frames AI assistants inside E2EE as breaking its guarantees absent strict technical conditions and consent — i.e., the research community is defining the bar a legitimate design must meet (https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20231).
- **NYT v. OpenAI discovery**: May 2025 court order forced OpenAI to preserve all consumer chat logs indefinitely; **only Enterprise and Zero-Data-Retention API customers were excluded** (https://openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands/; https://www.terms.law/2025/11/12/openai-v-new-york-times-stopped-being-just-a-copyright-case-the-moment-the-court-turned-to-your-chatgpt-logs/). This is the single best concrete argument that where plaintext exists at a provider, it becomes discoverable — and that buyers already pay for architectures where it doesn't.
- Practitioner series "Missing Primitives for Trustworthy AI" (Sept 2025): agent traffic is TLS-only, decrypted "at terminating proxies, API gateways, load balancers, and within the internal service mesh"; calls for exactly the primitives wires built — application-layer encryption, identity binding, forward secrecy for agent comms (https://www.sakurasky.com/blog/missing-primitives-for-trustworthy-ai-part-1/).
- Confidential Computing Consortium (May 2026): agents already in production ahead of security architecture; attestation/TEEs as the complement for the "agent must decrypt to think" endpoint problem (https://confidentialcomputing.io/2026/05/20/your-ai-agents-are-already-in-production-your-security-architecture-isnt-ready/).
- Matrix's government business (25+ countries) proves sovereign/E2EE group comms is a real procurement category.

**Arguments it's theater:**
- An agent member must decrypt to function; if it runs on OpenAI/Anthropic/AWS, plaintext lands on that cloud anyway — E2EE moved the trust boundary, not eliminated it (EDRi: https://edri.org/our-work/artificial-insecurity-how-ai-tools-compromise-confidentiality/). Whittaker makes the same point from the other direction: the agent *is* the backdoor.
- The revealed preference of the two credible open competitors (Buzz, Centaur) is that **identity + audit + self-hosting sells now; E2EE didn't make their v1**. A2A's 100-company consensus settled on OAuth+mTLS.
- Enterprise procurement mostly buys compliance (SOC2, retention controls, ZDR contracts) rather than cryptographic guarantees; confidential computing/TEE attestation is emerging as the "protect data in use" answer without E2EE group semantics.
- Prompt injection: an E2EE group with an agent member leaks via the agent's own behavior — Whittaker's point that the crypto stays sound while the guarantee evaporates.

## Frank assessment

**Where iroh+epoch-roster is differentiated:**
- The intersection wires occupies — *p2p transport (iroh) + committed membership (Merkle roster) + epoch E2EE + agents as first-class key-holding members* — is genuinely unoccupied. iroh-gossip explicitly punts auth to the app layer and n0 isn't building it; local-first has the group-key theory (Keyhive/BeeKEM) but it's pre-alpha, unaudited, and transport-agnostic; MLS has audited crypto but hard-requires a central sequencer that fights gossip transports; Marmot has the right shape but rides Nostr's relay model and has no users; Buzz has the agents-with-keys story but zero confidentiality.
- The design's honest novelty is the *committed roster* (provable inclusion, auditable membership, head-only revocation) combined with epoch rotation over a sequencer-free-ish gossip transport. Nobody shipping today offers "cryptographic proof of who is in the room" — Buzz offers signed history on a relay the operator controls; Matrix offers homeserver-mediated membership; MLS offers it only via the DS's say-so.
- The Whittaker critique is the design brief, not a rebuttal problem: agents as explicit, minimal, revocable roster members is the only architecture that answers "the agent is a backdoor" — you can name the backdoor, scope it, and revoke it at an epoch boundary.

**Where it's redundant / at risk:**
- Reimplementing group AKE outside MLS forfeits RFC-grade analysis and two audited Rust libraries. The defensible position is "our ordering model isn't MLS-shaped" (gossip, roster-derived membership) — the MLS community itself forks the protocol in this regime (DMLS, Germ), so this argument holds, but you inherit the MLS operational checklist: one-commit-per-epoch discipline, fork detection/recovery, key-deletion hygiene, and an explicit account of what any sequencing point learns.
- If the market decides identity+audit+self-hosting is enough (the Buzz/Centaur bet), the E2EE layer becomes a nice-to-have you paid a large complexity tax for.

**Biggest "why not just use X" threat: Block's Buzz.** Same shape (group chat for teams + agents, per-member cryptographic identity, decentralized-flavored, open source, self-hostable), Block-scale distribution, Goose integration, 27.6k stars in a month. The one-line answer exists — Buzz is signed plaintext on an operator-controlled relay; Wires is E2EE with provable membership and revocation — but Buzz could add Marmot/MLS later (Dorsey funds White Noise's ecosystem), so the window is real but not permanent. Second threat, different axis: **XMTP** ("MLS groups with agents as first-class members" already shipping in Base App) — mitigated by its crypto-wallet framing, 7-permissioned-node "decentralization," and tiny non-crypto traction. Third: **plain Slack+Centaur/A2A inertia** — the null hypothesis that enterprises just keep agents in Slack.

## The 5 facts the product decision should hinge on

1. **Buzz launched July 21, 2026 with the exact wires market shape and no E2EE** — 27.6k stars validates "group chat for humans+agents with cryptographic identity" as a category, and its plaintext-relay architecture is the precise gap an epoch-keyed roster fills (https://github.com/block/buzz).
2. **MLS won the centralized world and cannot follow into gossip**: every production MLS deployment has a provider-run sequencer; decentralized MLS is research-grade (DMLS, Germ pairwise), and Matrix has failed to land MLS since 2023 — so a sequencer-free epoch-roster design is defensible engineering, not NIH (https://blog.phnx.im/making-mls-more-decentralized/, http://arewemlsyet.com/).
3. **iroh 1.0 (June 15, 2026) de-risked the transport but iroh-gossip is 0.x with no auth and no delivery guarantees** — the wires roster layer is exactly what n0 punts to applications, and their relay pricing/EOL calendar (free support for old pins ends 2026) is now a dependency to manage (https://www.iroh.computer/blog/v1, https://docs.iroh.computer/connecting/gossip).
4. **The NYT v. OpenAI preservation order is the buyer-side proof that provider-held plaintext becomes discoverable** — and that "Zero Data Retention" exclusions are already a paid enterprise category; this is the strongest non-theoretical argument that E2EE-shaped guarantees for agent conversations have commercial pull (https://openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands/).
5. **Signal — the most trusted E2EE brand — has publicly vacated the agent use case**, with Whittaker calling agents-in-messengers a backdoor and prompt injection the coming exploit path (June 2026): simultaneously the market vacuum wires would fill and the critique the roster/revocation model must visibly answer (https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-meredith-whittaker-weekend-interview/).
